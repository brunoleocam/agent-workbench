# Microcopy

Interface text is part of the design. Generic copy makes a careful layout look generated. These rules apply to labels, headings, empty states, errors, and marketing sections.

## Buttons and links

- Use the product's verb and object: "Confirm tomorrow's visits", "Export March invoices". Avoid "Get started", "Learn more", "Submit", and "Click here" on their own.
- One primary action per view. Its label states the result.
- A label never wraps to two lines at any width from 320px to 1920px. Shorten the words, not the font size.

## Headings

- Portability test: if the heading could move unchanged to another product, it is filler. "Built for the modern team" fails; "Every vet appointment for tomorrow on one screen" passes.
- State what the user gets, in the user's words, not the company's.
- No colon reveals ("The best part: it's fast") and no "not X, it's Y" contrasts.

## Words to drop

leverage, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, empower, unlock, revolutionize, supercharge, effortless, next-generation, game changer, "all-in-one", "at scale".

In Portuguese: alavancar, potencializar, revolucionar, sem esforço, de ponta, robusto, solução completa, "eleve o seu", "transforme a sua".

Also drop empty adverbs (simply, just, truly, realmente, simplesmente) unless they carry meaning.

## Errors

Say what happened, why when known, and what to do next. Never blame the user, and never show a code alone.

- Bad: "Invalid input."
- Good: "The date is in the past. Pick a day from tomorrow on."

Put the error next to the field it concerns, and keep the user's input.

## Empty states

An empty screen is the first screen many users see. Say what will appear there and offer the first action: "No visits scheduled for tomorrow. Add the first one." Do not fill it with sample data or illustrations that say nothing.

## Success

When the result is already visible, stay silent: no toast for something the user can see. Confirm only what is invisible ("Reminder sent to 12 clients").

## Numbers, names, and quotes

- No invented metrics, testimonials, logos, or customer counts. Use real ones or none.
- No "John Doe", "Jane Doe", or "Acme". Sample names come from the brief's domain and are marked as samples.

## Self-check before delivery

1. Does every label name a concrete action or result?
2. Does every heading pass the portability test?
3. Is any banned word left?
4. Does every error say what to do next?
5. Is any number, name, or quote invented?
